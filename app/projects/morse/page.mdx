## TL;DR

LLM-based multi-agent systems mostly tell agents apart through role prompts on one shared, frozen model. Without explicit subtask assignment, agents end up with overlapping responsibilities and correlated outputs, and a single shared model cannot meet the distinct demands of the many subtasks inside one complex task.

**MoRSE** specializes every agent by its **(role, subtask)** pair at two levels: the *task structure* (which part of the task each agent owns) and the *parameters* (which LoRA experts each agent call uses). A hierarchical policy optimization trains the experts and their router stably from sparse task rewards.

## Why agents need to specialize

To motivate MoRSE's design, we ran two diagnostics: how redundant the outputs of collaborating agents are, and whether a single shared adapter can meet the demands of every role and subtask.

## Two levels of specialization

Every agent call in MoRSE carries a (role, subtask) label. The label is used twice: to decide *what* the agent works on, and to decide *which parameters* it runs with.

## Training experts and router together

Updating the experts and the router with one shared advantage mixes two questions: was this answer good for the experts that produced it, and was this expert combination a good choice? At each node, **HGRPO** samples $B$ routes and $M$ candidates per route, and answers the two questions with separate advantages:

$$
A_{\text{within}}^{(b,m)} = \frac{u^{(b,m)} - \mu^{(b)}}{\sigma^{(b)} + \epsilon}
$$

$$
A_{\text{cross}}^{(b)} = \frac{\mu^{(b)} - \bar{\mu}}{\sigma_C + \epsilon}
$$

Here $u^{(b,m)}$ is the step reward of candidate $m$ on route $b$, $\mu^{(b)}$ and $\sigma^{(b)}$ are the mean and standard deviation of the rewards within route $b$, $\bar{\mu}$ is the mean of the route means, and $\sigma_C$ is their standard deviation. The within-route advantage updates only the LoRA experts and the cross-route advantage updates only the router.

<CreditAssignment />

HGRPO keeps the expected gradient of standard GRPO while provably lowering the variance of the expert gradient: the within-route baseline removes the cross-route reward variance $\sigma_C^2$ from it.

## Results

We evaluate on two code-generation benchmarks: **SRDD** (1,200 software requirements in 40 categories) and **SciCode** (80 scientific computing problems with 338 unit-tested subproblems). Baselines are a single agent and three multi-agent systems, ChatChain, MacNet, and AFlow, all on the same backbone with the same prompts, decoding settings, and revision budget.
